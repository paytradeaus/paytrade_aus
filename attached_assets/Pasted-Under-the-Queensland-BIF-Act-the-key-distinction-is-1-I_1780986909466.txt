Under the **Queensland BIF Act**, the key distinction is:

## 1. If the trustee / respondent intends to pay the claim in full

They **do not need to issue a payment schedule**, provided they pay the full claimed amount **on or before the payment due date**. The Act says a respondent is not required to give a payment schedule if the amount claimed is paid in full by the due date. ([Queensland Legislation][1])

So in your example:

| Claim received | Payment due date | If paying in full                                                        |
| -------------- | ---------------: | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 9 May          |           9 June | No payment schedule required, but the full amount must be paid by 9 June |

The QBCC guidance says the same thing: if you agree with the claimed amount, simply pay the full amount by the due date. ([QBCC][2])

## 2. If the trustee / respondent intends to pay less than the claim

They must give the subcontractor a **payment schedule** by the earlier of:

1. the time required under the contract; or
2. **15 business days after the payment claim is given**. ([Queensland Legislation][1])

The payment schedule must identify the claim, state the amount proposed to be paid, and if less than claimed, give the reasons for withholding or reducing payment. ([Queensland Legislation][1])

Using your example, assuming Queensland business days and no relevant public holiday in the count:

| Claim received | 15 business day deadline | Payment due date | If paying less                                                       |
| -------------- | -----------------------: | ---------------: | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 9 May 2026     |              29 May 2026 |      9 June 2026 | Payment schedule due by 29 May, then scheduled amount paid by 9 June |

If the example is **9 May 2025**, the 15-business-day deadline would be **30 May 2025**.

## 3. What if no payment schedule is given?

If no valid payment schedule is given in time, and the full amount is not paid by the due date, the respondent becomes liable to pay the **full amount claimed** on the due date. ([Queensland Legislation][1])

So in your example, if no payment schedule is issued by the 15-business-day deadline and the contractor does not pay in full by **9 June**, the unpaid amount becomes recoverable/adjudicable as the full claimed amount.

## 4. Project trust account overlay

The project trust framework does **not replace** the BIF Act payment schedule timing. If the subcontractor is a project trust beneficiary, the payment must be made from the project trust account to the beneficiary’s nominated financial institution account. ([Queensland Legislation][1])

There is also a **post-payment notice** obligation: when a withdrawal is made from the project trust account to pay a subcontractor beneficiary, the trustee must give the beneficiary a notice of the withdrawal within **5 business days after making the withdrawal**, unless there is a reasonable excuse. ([Queensland Legislation][1])

So the clean compliance rule is:

**Pay full:** no payment schedule required; pay by due date; then issue project trust withdrawal/payment notice within 5 business days after payment.
**Pay less:** payment schedule within 15 business days, or earlier contract period; pay scheduled amount by due date; then issue project trust withdrawal/payment notice within 5 business days after payment.
