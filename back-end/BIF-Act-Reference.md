## 1. The Trust Account Framework (Chapter 2 of the BIF Act)

The BIF Act establishes a **trust account framework** to protect progress
payment and retention amounts for contractors and subcontractors in the building
and construction industry. There are two account types:

- **Project Trust Account (PTA)** — holds progress payments for the head
  contractor and the subcontractors it engages on an eligible project.
- **Retention Trust Account (RTA)** — holds cash retention amounts withheld
  under eligible contracts.

The framework was rolled out in phases. It currently applies to:

- **Eligible Queensland Government contracts of $1 million or more**; and
- **Private sector, local government, statutory authority and government-owned
  corporation contracts of $10 million or more.**

(Source: Qld Dept of Housing & Public Works — Trust account framework.)

### When is a Project Trust Account required? ("project trust work")

When determining whether a project trust account is required, the **head
contractor must assess whether more than 50% of the contract's value is for
project trust work**. (Source: QBCC — Project trust work.)

"Project trust work" (relating to a building or swimming pool) includes:

- **Construction work relating to a building** — erection/construction;
  renovations, extensions or repairs; lighting and electrical; water supply,
  sewerage or drainage; heating, ventilation and air-conditioning; installing
  prefabricated components; laying wet pour rubber.
- **Related site work** — site work related to construction work (e.g. retaining
  walls); soil testing and site classification; earthmoving and excavation;
  scaffolding erection, management and removal.
- **Specialist work** — preparing plans/specifications; work by an architect,
  registered professional engineer or surveyor; mechanical services work; fire
  protection work; termite inspection/treatment; contract administration (where
  they also designed the building).
- **Inspection and advice** (when associated with the building) — building
  inspections; building certification; energy efficiency assessments; fire
  safety advice.
- **Other** — swimming pool construction, installation, alteration and repair
  (whether or not associated with a building).

There are additional eligibility criteria beyond the type of work; the QBCC
publishes a trust account tool to confirm whether a specific contract requires a
trust account.

## 2. Retention Trust Accounts (RTA)

- An RTA is required where **cash retention is withheld** under an eligible
  contract; retention amounts must be held in trust for the party entitled to
  them rather than mixed with general business funds.
- **Failing to release a retention amount** to the contracted party in
  accordance with the contract, without a reasonable excuse, attracts a
  **maximum penalty of 200 penalty units or 1 year's imprisonment**. This also
  applies to failing to release other securities, such as a bank guarantee.
  (Source: Qld Dept of Housing & Public Works — Retention requirements; QBCC Act
  Part 4A and the BIF Act.)

## 3. Right to Progress Payments (Chapter 3 of the BIF Act)

### s 70 — Right to progress payments

> From each reference date under a construction contract, a person is entitled to
> a progress payment if the person has carried out construction work, or supplied
> related goods and services, under the contract.

### s 68 — Meaning of "payment claim"

A **payment claim** for a progress payment is a written document that:

1. identifies the construction work, or related goods and services, the progress
   payment relates to;
2. states the amount (the **"claimed amount"**) claimed as payable by the
   respondent;
3. requests payment of the claimed amount; and
4. includes any other information prescribed by regulation.

The claimed amount may include amounts owed under s 98(3) or amounts held under
the contract that the claimant claims are due for release. A written document
bearing the word **"invoice"** is taken to satisfy the "request for payment"
requirement (s 68(3)).

### s 75 — Making a payment claim

- A person (the **claimant**) who is, or claims to be, entitled to a progress
  payment may give a payment claim to the person (the **respondent**) who is or
  may be liable to make the payment.
- Unless it relates to a final payment, a payment claim must generally be given
  **before the end of the longest of**: any period worked out under the contract,
  or **6 months** after the construction work was last carried out / related
  goods and services were last supplied.

## 4. Responding to a Payment Claim

### s 76 — Responding to payment claim (payment schedules)

- If given a payment claim, a respondent **must respond by giving the claimant a
  payment schedule** within whichever period ends first:
  - the period (if any) within which the contract requires a payment schedule; or
  - **15 business days** after the payment claim is given.
  - **Maximum penalty: 100 penalty units.** (Also grounds for disciplinary action
    under the QBCC Act 1991.)
- A payment schedule is **not required** if the amount claimed is **paid in full
  on or before the due date** for the progress payment (s 76(2)).
- If a payment schedule is given, the respondent **must pay the amount proposed
  in the schedule no later than the due date** for the progress payment
  (s 76(3); maximum penalty 100 penalty units), subject to amounts the respondent
  is required to retain.

### s 77 — Consequences of failing to give a payment schedule

> If a respondent does not respond to a payment claim with a payment schedule as
> required under s 76, the respondent is **liable to pay the full amount claimed**
> to the claimant on the due date for the progress payment.

### s 100 — Recovering an unpaid amount as a debt

In court proceedings to recover an unpaid amount as a debt, the respondent is
**not entitled to bring a counterclaim or to raise any defence in relation to
matters arising under the construction contract** — the security-of-payment
regime is designed to keep cash flowing quickly down the contracting chain.

## 5. Other Security-of-Payment Protections

Alongside trust accounts, the framework provides further protections for getting
paid, including: **adjudication**, **payment withholding requests**, **charges
over property**, **subcontractors' charges**, and **monies-owed complaints**.

---

## 6. Key Definitions & Practical Points

- **Claimant** — the party entitled (or claiming to be entitled) to a progress
  payment (typically a subcontractor or head contractor).
- **Respondent** — the party who is, or may be, liable to make the payment.
- **Claimed amount** — the amount stated as payable in a payment claim.
- **Due date** — the date the progress payment is payable, worked out under the
  contract or, failing that, the default period set by the Act.
- **Penalty unit** — a dollar value set by Queensland regulation and indexed over
  time; multiply by the number of penalty units to estimate a maximum fine.
- Trust accounting obligations (record-keeping, account reviews, withdrawals only
  for permitted purposes) sit on top of these payment rules and are enforced by
  the QBCC.
